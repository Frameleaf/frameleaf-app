A smart album fills itself. There are two kinds: albums you build from your own rules, and six built-in categories that an administrator turns on.

[pause]

Start with your own. Choose New, then Smart album, and name it. The Smart album switch is already on, with the rules below.

Tick people and tags; any one of each is enough. Add a date range and choose photos, videos or both. The line underneath counts what matches right now, and nothing changes while you edit.

Looks like adds visual categories such as lake or trail, matched by smart search, with a Confidence slider. With these, the count comes from your newest items, and it says so.

When something matches decides what happens. Suggest for review waits for you. Add rule-owned tags adds matches to the album with the rule's own tag. Archive matches stays off unless you switch it on and agree.

[pause]

Choose Create. Nothing is matched until you apply the rule, so choose Re-evaluate. It shows what matches now, what would be added or suggested, and what no longer matches. Choose Apply.

Suggestions wait for you. Choose the suggestions link in the header, tick the photos that belong and choose Accept, or choose Reject. Rejected items never come back.

Open a photo, and the Smart albums line on its information card says why it is there: added by the rule, suggested, or kept by you.

[pause]

Now the built-in categories. An administrator opens Settings, then Search & intelligence, then Categories & smart albums, and turns on Build curated smart albums. Six albums are created for every account.

Travel, Documents, Screenshots, Food, Pets and Nature each have Tag triggers, one per line, matched against description tags. Add golden retriever to Pets, and Max is never missed.

CLIP queries match by meaning through smart search. Tag threshold starts at zero point two eight; lower it if an album is sparse, raise it if it is noisy.

Save, then choose Re-evaluate this album, or Re-evaluate all assets for every category. Only described images are checked.
